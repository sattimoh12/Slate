Your mother’s family · Ja’aliyin · Metemma, River Nile State

Abbas of Metemma

The family tree of your mother’s father, Abbas son of Abdalla son of Osman son of Isa son of Mohamed son of Hamed, and the two ancestral streams that meet in it.

In short. Your mother’s family were Ja’aliyin of Metemma, on the west bank of the Nile opposite Shendi. Their male line was also called Hadarbi, the name for merchants of Hadramawt origin who came up from Suakin to trade at Shendi. The family remembers six generations, back to Hamed, born around 1770–1800. Abbas worked with livestock and married a woman of the Baqlab. The family has always identified as Ja’aliyin of Metemma, with no Dongola connection. Your mother met your father, of the Satti family of Old Dongola, in Khartoum.

02 · What each generation lived through

The family’s history, year by year

1558–1821

The Ja’aliyin meks rule Metemma and Shendi

Metemma is the seat of the Nafi‘ab; Shendi, across the river, belongs to the Sa‘dab.

Before Hamed
1814

Burckhardt visits Shendi

He finds Hadarba merchants from Suakin trading Indian cloth, spices and sandalwood, and describes the Ja’aliyin as herders roaming the Butana.

Hamed
1822

Mek Nimr kills Isma‘il Pasha at Shendi

The Turkish reprisals that followed devastated Shendi and Metemma. Mek Nimr fled to the Ethiopian border and founded a second Metemma there.

Hamed · Mohamed
1885

Battle of Abu Klea and the fall of Khartoum

The British relief column fought its way across the desert to the Nile at Metemma. The Mahdi made Omdurman his capital.

Isa · Osman
1 July 1897

Kitlat al-Metemma (the Metemma killing)

Mahmud wad Ahmad sacks Metemma; over 2,000 Ja’aliyin die. Women and children are taken to Omdurman.

Osman
1898–1956

Anglo-Egyptian rule

Families rebuild at Metemma; many Ja’aliyin also move to Omdurman and Khartoum.

Abdalla
1956–

Independent Sudan

Abbas works with livestock and marries into the Baqlab. Your mother later meets your father in Khartoum.

03 · The key witness

Burckhardt at Shendi, 1814

“The most substantial of all the traders who at present frequent the Shendy market are the people from Souakin, or as they are more commonly called in this part of Africa, the Hadharebe, or Hadharame, that is, people of Hadremaut, in South Arabia, from whence they draw their origin. Some of these traders are always found at Shendy … no month passes without some arrivals from that quarter.”J. L. Burckhardt, Travels in Nubia (1819), p. 319
